Attributes, sometimes referred to as "stats", are the basic strength of your character but in Mini Legion, where those stats come from is extremely convoluted and hard to figure out so hopefully this guide will shed some light on the sources of attributes and how they are calculated.
There are 2 types of bonuses that add to your attributes:
Stats & Attributes
Static bonuses: A set amount of a particular attribute.
Dynamic bonuses: A percentage added onto your total static bonus.
Primary Attributes - Intellect, Agility, Strength
These are the primary attributes and come from the following sources:
Static
Base stats: At level 60, you will have 202 of your class’s primary attribute and 115 for the other 2. For example, a level 60 priest has base stats of 202 Int, 115 Agi, and 115 Str. (Note: I realize 202 and 115 seem like strange base numbers so it’s possible I missed a source of static int. Please let me know if you find another.)
Skill levels: As you level up your skills, the first and second passive bonuses can give primary stats. They are not summarized anywhere and have to be manually added together. (See first image.)
Collections & Gear
Gear collection sets: If you complete the 2 and 3 star set bonuses, you can earn some static primary bonuses. (See second image.)
Stats & Attributes
Train camp: Every 5 upgrades you get a bonus that provides +2 to each of the 3 primary stats. These are not summarized anywhere and have to be added manually. (See third image.)
Heirlooms: Every heirloom gives a static bonus to all primary stats. This is summarized on the heirloom screen.  (See fourth image.)
Gear, refine attributes, and set bonuses: The numbers you see on each item you equip.

Dungeon Farming
Mini Legion is built on a simple loop: AFK farming supplies most XP/loot, while dungeons/raids/solo challenges are where you convert that loot into real power. If progress feels slow, it’s usually not your level—it’s your efficiency. AFK leveling (fastest path): Pick a spot with high mob density, short travel, and low death risk. Your goal is kills per hour, not “hardest mobs.” If you’re dying or fights are dragging, step down slightly in difficulty and regain nonstop kills. Run two setups:
Character Builds
AFK build = survival + sustain so you farm longer without interruptions.
Instance build = damage + control so dungeons and bosses end faster. One “middle” build often feels weak everywhere.
Dungeon Farming
Upgrade smart: Put resources into upgrades that change outcomes: faster kill speed (often weapon first) and fewer deaths. Don’t spread materials evenly across everything. Fix inventory early: Set auto-dismantle/dissolve rules so low-value gear doesn’t clog your bag. Process loot in batches (don’t stop every minute), keep only upgrades and build-relevant pieces. Transmog (short for transmogrification) is a system that lets you change the appearance of your equipment without affecting your stats. You can make your gear look like any piece you've collected, keeping your powerful stats while looking exactly how you want.

Your appearance collection grows as you find and collect gear pieces throughout the game. Once you obtain a piece of equipment, its appearance is permanently added to your collection. You can then use that appearance on any compatible gear slot while keeping your current stats intact.

Overview
The Echo & Relic system is your main PvP progression path. There are 8 Echos total, all unlocked through PvP activities. Upgrading them unlocks powerful Relic bonuses that affect both PvP and PvE content.
The Echo screen showing all 8 Echos arranged around your Relic with current stats
Each Echo can be upgraded in two ways: Level (using Lightbreak Ash) and Quality (using Chaotic Crystals). All 8 Echos should be upgraded evenly to meet Relic threshold requirements. Focus on keeping them balanced rather than maxing one at a time.
Used to upgrade Echo levels. Obtained from: Playing PvP matches, PvP Shop purchases, and Weekly Packs.
Used to upgrade Echo quality. Obtained exclusively from Weekly Rank Rewards based on your PvP ranking.

Weekly Packs offering Lightbreak Ash bundles for faster progression

Priest Guide - Levelling and Cloudland - LITTLE SPENDER
Hello fellow friends. This is Jon Sao, who started playing in December 22nd and just purchased the Elf Supplies ($5), the Dawnbreaker package ($15), the Beast’s Eye trinket ($1) and when I reached Lv.40 the Heirloom Chest ($15), so I’m what is called a little spender, with less than $40 invested. My shirt is just level 150 green 5 stars, working towards getting upstarred to blue next week with Arena/Rares/events. So I hope this guide serves to fellow Priests wanting to level up to 60 and become a main healer later on but not wanting to spend $1000+ to get that fancy Red Shirt. Currently I’m just at Lv.52 30k CP, so this is not an endgame guide (you have a great guide for that endgame right here at the Discord), this is just a levelling one with focus onto doing Cloudland, Arena and all the side events that grants more perks to enhance levelling faster. I currently have 4 builds for the different stuff I’m doing: AFK, Boss, Adds and Heal. They are very self-explanatory, but I will go further on them:
AFK This one is for levelling overnight, when you are working/studying, with the family/friends and all those moments where you are not actively playing. You just place your Priest in a good levelling place (refer to the guide for best places) and hit AFK. The main focus with this build is just to reduce the incoming damage, since later on food is a ENORMOUS gold sink, so I prioritise receiving less damage and save some food more than doing a little more damage. If gold is not an issue you can swap some defensive skill for an offensive one (explained later) that can grant a minor boost to XP/min For the defense we have PV: Fortitude, Holy Fire, PV: Shield and Renew. The first three are staples and reduce the incoming damage a lot, saving up food. The later one, Renew, is the one I mentioned you can swap for Vampiric Embrace or Shadowy Apparitions. Renew consumes a lot of mana, so it’s a damage loss because sometimes your toon will have to wait couple of seconds for regenerate that mana and cast a damaging spell. But is a lot of healing so it lets you go to a more difficult camp earlier so that’s more XP per kill. This is personal preference and I would suggest to try one skill, AFK for 10 minutes, log back, check, swap it, AFK for another 10 minutes and then compare your XP/min and food consumption and decide for yourself. For attacking there are some staples too, like Holy Shock and Shadowy Vortex, the main Area of Effect (AoE) we have. Those are top priority so that’s why they are that high. Then you fulfil the skill bar with the next two damaging spells, SV: Gone and Shadowflame. The last two skills are the personal choice as I already said, with Renew, Vampiric Embrace and Shadowy Apparitions. Or even Psychic Horror, is a 3 seconds “shield” every 30 seconds, so like a 10% mitigation. You can try all the combinations for those last 2 slots between those 4 skills. It will change every level and CP rating.
About Talents, it is very simple. Your focus should be on the Shadowy Vortex and Holy Shock ones, those are the main priority. Suffering makes Holy Shock apply to SV: Hurt, but until that point since you are not casting Hurt at all, there is no reason to have those Talents at lower levels. Gear Replacement Let's make one thing very clear. Higher GS and little green arrows does not always mean you should upgrade gear. It can sometimes be misleading and actually lead to a loss of power and dps. One of the biggest things that the game does not take into account with individual gear upgrades is for the potential loss of set bonuses that may occur due to swapping out a piece. So you have to use caution when tossing new items into the mix. A lot of times the game will also consider a new item an upgrade simply because one stat, even if it is irrelevant for you, is higher than a current stat. For example, you can have a piece with good versatility and crit on it, but if a new piece has higher dodge on it than either of your existing stats, the game is likely to recommend it as an upgrade. You equip it and will actually see that you lose CP. So just pay attention when you are clicking through all those little green arrows to not replace a solid piece of gear based on the game's recommendation. With that in mind, let’s look at our stats so you feel better prepared and can make the right decision.
STAT PRIO As always, we are stuck behind the traditional hit and expertise caps, both of which will continue to increase for new content, but should remain the same for older content. So for raid and Mythic + dungeons you want to have a minimum of 20% hit and 15% expertise. If you have more than that it’s perfectly fine as we have already seen an increase in these requirements with the MC Abyss raids up to “expertise is 20% for abyss, 25% for mc3 last boss btw.” -s2.CodeGreen. One of the best ways to do this is to push as many of your collections items for hit and expertise that still pertain to your “Main” class. Ie Agility for hunters. Since all classes will need to meet these thresholds it only makes sense to do that here and supplement the rest of the hit and expertise from your gear. Once we have met the minimum thresholds for hit and expertise, our stat priority is: Versatility > Crit > Hit > Expertise While you can reforge a piece of gear to hope for bis stats, it is quite expensive so sometimes it is easier to wait for a different piece to drop and use the heritage to move your affixes over to it. If a piece has versatility and any of the other 3 attributes I’m pretty happy with it. Top plays will be versatile and critical.

Why versatility over crit chance? Versatility is just that good. For every percentage that you increase versatility, you increase every point of damage by that same percentage. You even get the added bonus of .5% damage reduction on top of it. Survivability matters! Where crit on the other hand is a percent chance that you will do a flat 150% damage. On a point by point basis, you would need a 100% crit chance to match 50% versatility. That’s not to say that crit is useless as most classes, esp hunter, have some core talents that synergize very heavily with our ability to crit. (We’ll dive into those in a bit)

Light Brotherhood Reputation Farm Guide
Unlike other reputations, which are gained easily through afk farming in world zones, the Light Brotherhood reputation is less obvious and slower to obtain yet crucially important if you want to hit exalted for your heirloom boots, Dawncharger mount, and level 60 trinket. While you cannot AFK farm this rep like the others, it is still possible to obtain it quickly if you are intentional about it. Reputation Sources 1) Zone Questing 2) Repeatable Quest 3) Elites 4) Dungeons 5) Raids 6) Diamond Purchase for Alts ---------------------------------------------- 1) Zone Questing If you complete all of the main and side quests Sicklands, you will end in the 2nd bubble of revered reputation but still have to farm 40-45k more rep. This is very slow to do just 10 rep at a time like methods 2 and 3 below. 2) Repeatable Quest The reputation vendor says there is a repeatable quest called "Dirge of Souls" but if you click it to go, it only takes you to a 2nd rep vendor. The actual quest is called "Soul's Lament" and the location is shown in the 1st image. If you check the 2nd image, you will see that this quest gives 10 reputation. Very slow, but if you are farming to level your train camp anyway, it will help some. 3) While you AFK farm, if you select the last 2 elites to kill, you should log back in to find 20-80 rep. Each of these elites (3rd photo), will grant you 10 rep per kill, but unfortunately they are on 3 hr and 3.5 hr respawn timers so it is not a viable way to farm quickly. 4) Dungeons All 6 of the final heroic dungeons will award 250 Light Brotherhood reputation in the chest dropped by each boss (See 4th image.)
Each dungeon costs the same, 21 dungeon tickets, but the first 4 award more rep.
5) Raids Both Blood Ritual 1 and 2 will award 375 rep per boss kill (5th image). Because of the speed to clear these raids and increased rep, combined with having 3 free attempts per week, this is by far the best place to earn rep. If you do all 3 raid attempts per week for each raid, for 7 bosses dropping 375 rep each, you get 3 x 7 x 375 = 7,875 rep per week. You can get more if you use your raid tickets as well. 6) Diamond Purchase for Alts Once your main has reached exalted, if you feel like speeding up the process for your alts to get their trinket, you can buy "Light Brotherhood's Mark" from the reputation vendor which costs 10 bound diamonds and awards 250 reputation usable by any of your characters. So it would cost around 1,600-1,800 bound diamonds to max an alt that has finished all of the quests. ---------------------------------------------- A player who has finished all Sicklands quests could reach exalted in one week if desired: Starting rep: Revered ~5000 Needed rep: ~45,000 Raid: 7,875 Dungeon x 24 (about 3 per day; 504 dungeon tickets): 36,000 Elites or Repeat Quests x 113: 1130 Total: 45,005 If you found this guide helpful, please give a thumbs up on the first post!

Stats & Attributes
Attributes, sometimes referred to as "stats", are the basic strength of your character but in Mini Legion, where those stats come from is extremely convoluted and hard to figure out so hopefully this guide will shed some light on the sources of attributes and how they are calculated. There are 2 types of bonuses that add to your attributes:
Static bonuses: A set amount of a particular attribute.
Dynamic bonuses: A percentage added onto your total static bonus.
Primary Attributes - Intellect, Agility, Strength These are the primary attributes and come from the following sources: Static
Base stats: At level 60, you will have 202 of your class’s primary attribute and 115 for the other 2. For example, a level 60 priest has base stats of 202 Int, 115 Agi, and 115 Str. (Note: I realize 202 and 115 seem like strange base numbers so it’s possible I missed a source of static int. Please let me know if you find another.)
Skill levels: As you level up your skills, the first and second passive bonuses can give primary stats. They are not summarized anywhere and have to be manually added together. (See first image.)
Collections & Gear
Gear collection sets: If you complete the 2 and 3 star set bonuses, you can earn some static primary bonuses. (See second image.)
Stats & Attributes
Train camp: Every 5 upgrades you get a bonus that provides +2 to each of the 3 primary stats. These are not summarized anywhere and have to be added manually. (See third image.)
Heirlooms: Every heirloom gives a static bonus to all primary stats. This is summarized on the heirloom screen. (See fourth image.)
Gear, refine attributes, and set bonuses: The numbers you see on each item you equip.
Dynamic
Skill levels: Some skills at max level give a dynamic bonus to primary stats. These are not summarized anywhere and have to be added manually. (See first image above again.)
Rune bonus: Gained from runes and summarized in “Total Rune Bonus”. (See first image.)
Racial passives: Nightelf – 5% agility, Orc – 5% strength, Undead – 5% Intellect. These are listed as a passive skill in your skill window.
Collections & Gear
Gear collection: Gained when you update gear collection items and summarized on the collection screen. (See second image.)
Gear collection sets: Gained when you complete a 5 star set bonus. (See second image above.)

Hello fellow Mini Legionists! Reading through the general channel I noticed that some questions pop up quite often and it may be difficult to answer them properly without screenshots. So here are some of the things you may be looking for or things you simply missed.
How to automatically dissolve items from afk farming?
You need to choose the right settings in the Dismantle -> Auto tab. Now you need to tick the Enable dismantle check in the AFK Backpack when collecting your afk loot. First time all the loot will be shown in the AFK Backpack but after clicking Claim All you will only receive gear crystals. After that you will be dismantling items as they drop.
My character doesn't stay where I left him for afk farming
You need to make sure that you actually left him for afk farming instead of letting him do whatever he wants. To do that make sure the marked icon shows Auto AFK. When afk farming you can also choose to farm Elite mobs - your character will move to the Elite location and come back to afk spot after killing. If you have a free teleport option (Dawnbreaker pack - highly recommended) your character will farm much more efficiently. If your character dies during afk farming he'll move to the recommended afk farm spot (recommended by the game, not the players)
You can find it in the Settings menu (accessible via the cogwheel icon on the right side of the screen)
How can I turn off the Meetstone notification spam"
Go to the main chat window and untick Meetstone check
Dungeon Farming
How can I solo farm dungeons with my alts?
You can add alts in the team view when making a dungeon team. Although it's recommended to have at least one more player in the team - optimal is 4 your characters and 1 other player. This way you will be presented with a choice of rewards after each boss. Going solo gives you no choice and has a chance to give 0 items.
Character Builds
My alts are using weird builds in the dungeon
You need to select the right build when adding your alt.
How can I remove alt from the dungeon team?
You can drop the item in the bank - city view. The Bank icon visible in your inventory shows only a personal stash of the current character. This one is a good option to keep alternative gear.

Gear Replacement Let's make one thing very clear. Higher GS and little green arrows does not always mean you should upgrade gear. It can sometimes be misleading and actually lead to a loss of power and dps. One of the biggest things that the game does not take into account with individual gear upgrades is for the potential loss of set bonuses that may occur due to swapping out a piece. So you have to use caution when tossing new items into the mix. A lot of times the game will also consider a new item an upgrade simply because one stat, even if it is irrelevant for you, is higher than a current stat. For example, you can have a piece with good versatility and crit on it, but if a new piece has higher dodge on it than either of you existing stats, the game is likely to recommend it as an upgrade. You equip it and will actually see that you lose CP. So just pay attention when you are clicking through all those little green arrows to not replace a solid piece of gear based on the game's recommendation. With that in mind, let’s look at our stats so you feel better prepared and can make the right decision.
STAT PRIO As always, we are stuck behind the traditional hit and expertise caps, both of which will continue to increase for new content, but should remain the same for older content. So for raid and Mythic + dungeons you want to have a minimum of 20% hit and 15% expertise. If you have more than that it’s perfectly fine as we have already seen an increase in these requirements with the MC Abyss raids up to “expertise is 20% for abyss, 25% for mc3 last boss btw.” -s2.CodeGreen. One of the best ways to do this is to push as many of your collections items for hit and expertise that still pertain to your “Main” class. Ie Agility for hunters. Since all classes will need to meet these thresholds it only makes sense to do that here and supplement the rest of the hit and expertise from your gear. Once we have met the minimum thresholds for hit and expertise, our stat priority is: Versatility > Crit > Hit > Expertise While you can reforge a piece of gear to hope for bis stats, it is quite expensive so sometimes it is easier to wait for a different piece to drop and use the heritage to move your affixes over to it. If a piece has versatility and any of the other 3 attributes I’m pretty happy with it. The best pieces are going to be versatility and crit though.
Character Builds
Why versatility over crit chance? Versatility is just that good. For every percentage that you increase versatility, you increase every point of damage by that same percentage. You even get the added bonus of .5% damage reduction on top of it. Survivability matters! Where crit on the other hand is a percent chance that you will do a flat 150% damage. On a point by point basis, you would need a 100% crit chance to match 50% versatility. That’s not to say that crit is useless as most classes, esp hunter, have some core talents that synergize very heavily with our ability to crit. (We’ll dive into those in a bit)
Tier Stats (Refinable) We have a LOT of options depending on the gear slot. The percent increases are ALWAYS the first thing I hunt for (no pun intended) when refining. Here is a breakdown of how I prioritize these: Helm; Shoulders; Chest; Legs;

If having Cunning Strike on your bar is reducing your DPS because you are mana starved, you may need to refine for % Mana Regen or be forced to purchase the epic version of the rune.

Mythic+ 25 Application and Advice SLOW DOWN. If you don’t wait for your tank to establish threat, your Blast Trap will 100% pull the pack to you and it’s GG. At M+ 25 the death penalty is 60s and can not be wasted on you being impatient. Let the tank gather the mobs and get off an AOE ability before you start going in on them. They will die. Secondly, stunning a boss may interrupt his current cast of the AOE, but he is just going to dish it right back out. Which means the entire team has now wasted time running out, getting faked out because you stunned the boss, then running back in, then the new AOE is coming down and you’re running out again. It is a waste of valuable time and DPS.
On a side note, the hunter behaves differently than the rogue's stun. It is really easy for the stun to not cast right away, whether that be the character moving into range, or from the GCD hanging and in all of our runs we haven’t quite pinpointed the exact chain of events, but two really weird things happen with hunter stuns. This is no bueno when this should be the safe time for your tank to build more threat, a chance for your healer to top of the group and even sneak in some damage, but now it’s panic mode because the boss is still going strong. The other, and by far scarier thing that can happen, which we have seen more times than I wish, is the boss casting an AOE with ZERO wind up and NO telegraphing. Something about stunning during the AOE animation can trigger this, so just avoid it all together. If the boss is enraged this is likely 2 to 3 deaths which could mean the difference between success and failure.
Find a rogue who is willing to take a DPS loss by playing Sub and learning how to chain stun. Having a boss stun locked during his enrage is a huge boon for the group. These have been some of the key pieces in our success as a group. Without any of these pieces of knowledge we would not have made it as far as we did.
